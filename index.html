Start here

No telescope required. These three habits will show you more of the sky than any piece of equipment will.

✦

Learn five stars first

Five bright anchors are enough to navigate the whole sky. We start with Vega, Arcturus, Sirius, Capella and Altair.

◐

Give your eyes 20 minutes

Dark adaptation is slow. Avoid white light, use a red torch, and the faint detail will quietly appear.

◎

Observe the same patch weekly

Familiarity beats novelty. Watching one constellation shift night by night teaches you how the sky moves.

What you actually need

You can spend a fortune on astronomy gear. You do not have to. Everything on this list costs less than a decent pair of shoes.

We test beginner kits every season and only recommend the ones we would hand to a friend.

  • Binoculars (10×50) — the single best first purchase.
  • A red torch — protects your night vision.
  • A star chart or app — printed charts never run out of battery.
  • A folding chair — comfort keeps you outside longer.
  • Warm layers — the clearest nights are always the coldest.
